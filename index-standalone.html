What is Indian Status?

Indian Status is federal registration under the Indian Act. It recognizes certain First Nations people for specific legal rights, programs, and services. It is not a government-issued measurement of culture, ancestry, or belonging.

The word “Indian” appears here because it remains the legal term used by the Indian Act and in the name “Indian Status.”

Myth and reality

A family line is not a legal formula.

Myth“Once Status enters a family, it passes down forever.”
RealityLegal entitlement can end inside a living family tree.

Kim Wheeler explains that a registered parent may have a registered child, while the following generation may not qualify if that child later has a child with someone who is not entitled to registration (Wheeler). The government’s line can stop. The family line does not.

What the formula cannot carry

The paperwork changes.
Their relationship does not.

A federal register can decide whom Canada recognizes under one law. It cannot turn ancestry, kinship, community, or Nation into a switch that clicks off.

The rule underneath the myth

Two sections.
Different transmission.

Section 6(1) can transmit entitlement when the other parent is not entitled. Section 6(2) can transmit entitlement only when the other parent is also entitled (Indigenous Services Canada).

People registered under 6(1) and 6(2) can access the same status-linked rights, programs, and services. Their ability to transmit entitlement is different.

What survives the formula

Identity outlives the formula.

The status system can shrink a legal population without changing the people. A federal register may decide whom Canada recognizes. It cannot make a child less connected to family, community, or Nation.
